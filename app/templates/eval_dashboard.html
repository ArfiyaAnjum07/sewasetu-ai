Design Note · The Decisions That Mattered Most

Key architectural choices made across the citizen and block officer MCP servers.

1

Strict Dual MCP Surface Separation

Decision: Completely separate HTTP endpoints (:8101 for Citizens, :8102 for Block Officers). Citizens authenticate via mobile+OTP; Officers via departmental credentials. A citizen assistant cannot even see officer inspection or decision tools.

Why it mattered: Prevents privilege escalation and prompt injection where an LLM could hallucinate officer approval permissions. Enforced cryptographically by RS256 portal tokens with audience & role matching.

2

Portal as Single Source of Truth

Decision: Reused authoritative domain logic (validate_application and create_application) directly from the portal app. The MCP server does not reimplement scheme rules or validation constraints independently.

Why it mattered: Guarantees zero policy drift. As the Director emphasized: an AI assistant is just another channel to apply; it must never grant a pension that the counter or paper form would refuse.

3

Defensive Normalization for Small Models

Decision: Implemented input tolerance in the tool layer for date formats (ISO YYYY-MM-DD → DD/MM/YYYY), block name casing, and bank account spacing, while maintaining strict invariant enforcement.

Why it mattered: Evaluators test with Claude Code running small models with tight turn limits. Failing valid 70-year-old citizens over whitespace or date formatting degrades real-world public welfare access.

4

What We Would Do Differently With More Time

Decision to refine: Age-proof document OCR and automated liveness verification at submission time.

Why: Currently, the assistant accepts document references or creates verified stub declarations. In production, an on-device lightweight OCR pipeline (extracting birth year directly from Aadhaar/Voter ID photos) would catch blurry uploads before they hit the officer queue.

What Evals Found & What We Changed

Direct failures discovered during early evaluations and the code modifications implemented in response.

1. Date Format Hallucinations

Discovery: Small models frequently transformed rural conversational dates (e.g. "born around flood of 1954") into ISO 8601 (1954-08-15) instead of the portal's strict DD/MM/YYYY requirement, causing immediate validation rejection.

Fix Applied: Added _normalize_dob() helper in citizen.py that transparently accepts ISO, hyphenated, and slash dates, formatting them to canonical DD/MM/YYYY before calling validate_application().

2. ASGI Header Detachment

Discovery: FastMCP 1.9.0's RequestContext dataclass lacked a direct .request.headers attribute, causing token claims extraction to fail with AttributeError.

Fix Applied: Developed HeaderCaptureMiddleware in common.py and a custom runner run_mcp_server() that captures HTTP headers from ASGI scope into a contextvars.ContextVar, ensuring 100% reliable bearer token validation.

3. Duplicate Apply Frustration

Discovery: When citizens with active pending applications attempted to re-apply with corrected bank details, the portal rejected them without explaining the withdrawal mechanism.

Fix Applied: Added withdraw_my_application tool to citizen MCP and clear guidance in apply_for_pension informing applicants of the statutory withdrawal-and-reapply flow.
